# Project: Amar Bogura — Admin Panel & Contributor Portal

## 1. Role and Responsibilities

Act as a Senior Software Architect, Senior Full-Stack Developer, UI/UX Designer, Database Architect and QA Engineer.

Develop a production-oriented Admin Panel and Contributor Portal for **Amar Bogura (আমার বগুড়া)**, a comprehensive district information and community platform for Bogura, Bangladesh.

Do not create only static mockups. Build functional interfaces, database models, APIs, authentication, authorization, validation, submission workflows and realistic demo data.

First inspect the existing repository and identify the current technology stack, coding conventions, existing components and implemented features. Preserve working code. Do not unnecessarily replace existing architecture.

If the repository is empty, follow the recommended architecture below.

---

## 2. Product Vision

Amar Bogura is a digital information platform that collects, verifies, manages and publishes reliable information about Bogura district.

The platform should cover:

- Administrative geography
- Upazilas, unions, municipalities, wards and villages
- Educational institutions
- Hospitals, clinics and diagnostic centres
- Government offices
- Tourism and historical places
- Rivers, canals and natural resources
- Markets, shops and businesses
- Agriculture and industries
- Transportation and infrastructure
- Blood donation and community services
- Local news, notices and events
- Jobs, rentals and local opportunities

Every important information record must have a source, location, verification status and update history.

# 7. Submission Workflow and Business Logic

Implement the following state transitions:

Draft → Submitted → Pending Review → Under Review

Under Review can transition to:

- Approved
- Revision Requested
- Rejected

Revision Requested → Resubmitted → Under Review

Approved → Published

Published → Correction Requested / Archived / Superseded

Important rules:

1. Only authenticated contributors can submit information.
2. Contributors can manage their own drafts and returned submissions.
3. Geographic scope restrictions must be enforced on the backend.
4. Category permissions must be enforced on the backend.
5. Duplicate detection must run before accepting a submission.
6. Required fields must be validated server-side.
7. Source/evidence requirements depend on the record category.
8. Contributors cannot approve their own records.
9. Only authorized publishers can publish approved records.
10. All important transitions must be recorded.
11. Published data must be versioned.
12. Rejected records must retain their rejection reasons.
13. Archived records must remain available for audit purposes.
14. Public APIs must return published records only.
15. Corrections to published records must not silently overwrite the previous published version.

Use database transactions for approval and publication operations.
